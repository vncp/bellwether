Two release binaries ran the same sections: **before** is `main` at `f2a7fa8`, and **after** is WP22b S1 at `79e82d3`. [How it ran](#how-it-ran) gives each section's start time, commit, and outside load.

All the after numbers are in [`2026-10-27-perf.json`](2026-10-27-perf.json), and all the before numbers in [`2026-10-27-before-perf.json`](2026-10-27-before-perf.json) (`bellwether-perf/1`). [`scripts/perf-report.py`](../../scripts/perf-report.py) prints every table marked "generated" from those files.

## Headline: plan time under full-scan time

WP22b's target is a plan p50 under the full-scan p50 on every corpus of the document. **It is met on no corpus.** Generated:

| corpus | cache | lookups whose plan p50 beat the scan | slowest plan p50 (ms) | full scan p50 (ms) | target |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| market-default | warm | 0 of 6 | 14.6 | 3.98 | not met |
| market-default | cold | 0 of 6 | 32.6 | 3.70 | not met |
| market-10x | warm | 16 of 18 | 46.1 | 39.6 | not met |
| market-10x | cold | 8 of 18 | 63.6 | 39.4 | not met |
| gh-events | warm | 0 of 18 | 34.3 | 4.94 | not met |
| gh-events | cold | 0 of 18 | 52.8 | 4.75 | not met |

- **market-10x, warm:** every lookup at 10 and 300 keys plans in 18.9 to 29.4 ms, under the 39.6 ms scan (12 of 12). At 3,000 keys, 4 of 6 plan under it (36.0 to 39.0 ms); the two 30-day windows take 45.9 and 46.1 ms. Before, these lookups took 58 ms to 7.4 s.
- **market-10x, cold:** 8 of 18. A cold plan rewrites its key set, two durable writes (H9 below).
- **market-default and gh-events:** no lookup plans under the scan, warm or cold. Their scans take 4.0 and 4.9 ms, and a 10-key plan has a floor of about 13.5 ms ([below](#the-small-corpus-floor)).
- **End to end** (plan, then reading the planned row groups through the bench's byte-counting reader), warm: 11 of 42 lookups now beat the full scan and 3 beat DuckDB on the same files (`latest` and `asof` at 300 keys, `latest` at 3,000 keys, all on `market-10x`). Before, 0 of 42 beat either, as in the 2026-10-26 document.

## The small-corpus floor

On `market-default` and `gh-events` a 10-key plan takes 13.5 to 14.6 ms warm, against a 4.0 to 4.9 ms scan. The profile `plan-floor-market-default-10` (200 runs of `bellwether latest` with 10 keys on `market-default`, [generated below](#plan-floor-market-default-10)) splits the main thread's wall time:

| share of main-thread wall time | what |
|---:|---|
| 60.7% | the durable write of the plan's Evidence record (`Ledger::put` → `put_overwrite`): 33.6% writing and syncing the staged file (`write_staging`), 24.9% syncing its directory (`sync_dir`); `sync_all` is `fcntl(F_FULLFSYNC)` on macOS |
| 12.2% | planning (`Planner::plan_ordered`) |
| 8.4% | parsing the manifest (`Manifest::from_slice`) |
| 6.0% | pinning the manifest (`Engine::pin`) |

Process start is 4.2 ms at p50 (`bellwether --version`, in the same run). With fsync disabled (the H9 pair below), the same plan takes 6.5 ms instead of 13.0 ms. So about half the floor is the durable evidence write, and even without it a plan stays over a 4 to 5 ms scan: process start, the manifest, and the staleness check alone come to more than the scan. On files this small, a full scan is the right plan; the index pays from `market-10x`'s size up.

Hypothesis numbers are those of [the 2026-10-26 document](2026-10-26-performance.md#hypotheses-for-wp22b-ranked-by-expected-gain); C1 to C4 came from re-profiling after H3.

## H9: the largest remaining candidate

Every durable write the local store makes syncs its file and then its directory (`fcntl(F_FULLFSYNC)` on macOS). With fsync disabled in an otherwise identical build, back to back, 11 builds and 31 plans each:

| command | with fsync | without | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `index` of `market-10x`, all threads, a fresh index | 751.2 ms | 135.4 ms | −82% |
| `index` of `market-default`, all threads, a fresh index | 91.7 ms | 28.4 ms | −69% |
| `latest`, 10 keys, `market-default` | 13.0 ms | 6.5 ms | −50% |

When the two binaries alternated run by run instead, the builds took 861.2 against 133.2 ms and 292.5 against 43.5 ms: each sync also waits on the other binary's writes, so the upper bound depends on what else writes to the disk. **It is not taken in S1**: syncing less changes the local store's durability contract (what a crash may lose, and whether a manifest can name a segment that did not reach the disk). That needs a store-durability ADR, with crash-consistency tests, before any code changes.

## Reconcile memory

[ADR 018's amendment](../adr/018-memory-is-bounded-per-operation.md) of 2026-10-27 makes the bound a bound on physical memory. The stated bound is the budget, plus one decoded batch (`peak_batch_bytes`), plus the 256 MiB footer cache. Batches are now at most 8,192 rows, so the bound itself is smaller after; each row uses its own run's batch. Peak footprint is `/usr/bin/time -l`'s peak memory footprint (dirty memory); peak RSS also counts clean mapped pages.

Computed from the two `perf.json` files (`reconcile/<pair>/<budget>/peak-footprint` and `peak-rss`, and the `memory` context the bound comes from):

| pair | budget | passes before / after | peak footprint before (MiB) | after | peak RSS before (MiB) | after | stated bound before / after (MiB) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ledger-small | 64M | 10 / 9 | 1,615.9 **(over)** | 71.6 | 1,625.5 **(over)** | 81.1 | 357.8 / 322.5 |
| ledger-small | 256M | 3 / 2 | 1,268.9 **(over)** | 220.7 | 1,305.2 **(over)** | 257.8 | 549.8 / 514.5 |
| ledger-small | 1G | 1 / 1 | 448.1 | 254.2 | 457.6 | 263.5 | 1,317.8 / 1,282.5 |
| ledger-small | 8G | 1 / 1 | 448.1 | 254.1 | 457.6 | 263.0 | 8,485.8 / 8,450.5 |
| taxi | 64M | 224 / 224 | 341.4 | 90.0 | 436.6 | 127.2 | 462.2 / 321.1 |

- **The small rung's defect is fixed.** At 64 MiB the footprint went from 1,615.9 MiB, 4.5 times its bound, to 71.6 MiB. Every budget on the rung is now under its bound on both measures, and so is every taxi budget up to 1 GiB.
- **Taxi at 8 GiB: peak RSS is 0.4% over the bound.** RSS reached 8,484.8 MiB against an 8,449.1 MiB bound, while the footprint (7,316.8 MiB) is under it. Before, RSS was 7,572.3 MiB, under a bound 141 MiB larger (its batch was a whole 142 MiB row group). Two more runs of each binary at 8 GiB alone (not merged into `perf.json`) gave the same peaks to within 0.01%: after, RSS 8,484.4 and 8,484.6 MiB and footprint 7,317.0 and 7,317.1 MiB; before, RSS 7,572.1 and 7,572.3 MiB and footprint 5,506.8 and 5,507.1 MiB. So the rise is not noise: at 8 GiB on taxi the footprint went up by 1,810 MiB (33%) and the wall time by 8 to 11%. The meter's peak is the same in both builds (4,955.4 MiB), so the extra memory is outside the accounted state; its cause is not isolated here, and it is an open item ([below](#storage-version-3-and-next-candidates)). The local budget for this metric is capped at the bound, so `bench perf-check` without `--runner` reports it until it is resolved; CI does not run taxi.

## Resident bytes per key

`tests/resident.rs` (contract v3, entry 32), in this run's release build, over 1,048,576 keys:

| build | open segment writer, `UInt64` keys | layout map, 9-byte keys |
|---|---:|---:|
| after (`79e82d3`) | **1,001.4 bytes** (111.3 × the 9 accounted) | 84.3 bytes |
| after without H5 (the writer of `f2a7fa8`) | 622.0 bytes (69.1 ×) | not rerun |
| 2026-10-26 document (`f809e54`) | 320 bytes (35.5 ×) | 84 bytes |

H5's hash map holds 379 more bytes per open key than the ordered map it replaced, so for narrow keys the resident memory of `specs × cap` is about 111 times the cap. The step from 320 to 622 bytes happened between `f809e54` and `f2a7fa8`, before WP22b; it was not bisected here. The build and replay READMEs and contract v3 state the new constant. Charging the writer's resident overhead to the caps remains a candidate (below).

## Storage version 3 and next candidates

No S1 change touches a persisted byte, so nothing here is a storage version 3 change yet.

- **H9 (fsync), the largest remaining candidate:** measured upper bound −82% on a `market-10x` build, −69% on a `market-default` build, and −50% on a 10-key plan ([H9](#h9-the-largest-remaining-candidate)); not taken in S1, it needs a store-durability ADR with crash-consistency tests.
- **The plan floor after H9:** parsing the manifest is 8.4% of a small plan's main-thread wall time and pinning it 6.0%; a smaller manifest is a candidate once H9 is decided.
- **H1, parallel reconcile passes,** unchanged from the 2026-10-26 document: reconcile still runs on one thread.
- **H2, the ledger rung's build** still uses about one worker: throughput is 18.5 row groups per second on one thread and 21.8 on all.
- **Resident overhead:** charging the segment writer's measured bytes per key to the caps (build and replay).
- **Open: reconcile on taxi at 8 GiB** holds 1,810 MiB more footprint than before, outside the meter, and its RSS is 0.4% over the stated bound ([Reconcile memory](#reconcile-memory)). It needs a heap profile of the two builds at that budget.

## Budgets

[`budgets.toml`](budgets.toml) is written by `scripts/perf-budgets.py` from [`2026-10-27-perf.json`](2026-10-27-perf.json), by the rule of [the 2026-10-26 document](2026-10-26-performance.md#budgets): every latency gets measured × 1.25 and every throughput measured ÷ 1.25, with the section's start time, commit, and outside load; a reconcile peak-memory budget is never above the stated bound.

- **The CI runner (`[runner]`).** From [`2026-10-27-runner-perf.json`](2026-10-27-runner-perf.json): the `perf` job of pull request #37 on `ubuntu-latest` (AMD EPYC 7763, 4 cores, 15 GiB), at the pull request's merge commit `074691fa789f`, which merges `79e82d3` into `f2a7fa8`. `perf.yml` checks every labeled pull request and every nightly run against it ([`docs/benchmark.md`](../benchmark.md#2026-10-27-the-budget-check-in-ci-and-the-regression-rule-wp22b)).
- **Loaded rows.** The budgets come from the after run, whose outside load peaked under 3 cores in every section. Its ledger section had a median of 2.1 cores outside (71% of its samples over 2), so the ledger budgets are loose by that much. Four before sections peaked over 3 cores (reconcile on the rung 3.0, `why` 3.2, the ledger 4.5, taxi 3.2); they are comparisons, not budgets.

## Not measured here

- **Cold-cache disk reads.** The OS page cache could not be dropped without root.
- **The DataFusion baseline.** It comes from the nightly runner; the `datafusion` feature is never built locally.
- **The multi-machine build (ADR 030).** It is parked, not part of S1.
- **Latency and profiles for the before binary.** Its release test builds would have needed a second release target directory; the replay p50 runs and observe latencies are in the 2026-10-26 document.
